Research Program

The Quantized Dimensional Ledger (QDL) proposes a structural dimensional-closure framework in which physical quantities are represented as integer vectors within a dimensional lattice. Admissible physical constructions must close on a conserved Quantized Dimensional Cell (QDC) in a 3L + 2F basis.

Framework

Dimensional Lattice

QDL embeds dimensional quantities into a discrete lattice structure where each physical quantity corresponds to an integer-exponent vector. Closure conditions restrict the set of admissible constructions.

Quantized Dimensional Cell

The conserved QDC acts as a structural invariant across dimensional transformations, allowing dimensional reasoning to be expressed in algebraic form.

Prediction Filtering

Model Admissibility

QDL can act as a pre-phenomenological filter on model construction by excluding terms that violate dimensional closure.

EFT Structure

The framework suggests that certain EFT operator structures may be restricted by dimensional closure constraints, potentially reducing the allowed operator basis.

Experimental Program

Torsion Balance Scaling

Precision torsion-balance experiments can probe dimensional-closure scaling relations in gravitational regimes.

Quantum Platforms

Experimental proposals include NV-center systems, cavity resonators, and metamaterials designed to test dimensional scaling relations.

Implications

Measurement Integrity

Dimensional closure offers a systematic approach for auditing measurement models and ensuring consistency across dimensional transformations.

Cross-Domain Applications

Beyond fundamental physics, QDL methods may be useful for validating engineering models, data pipelines, and complex system simulations.
