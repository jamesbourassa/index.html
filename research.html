QDL Research: Established Mathematics, Testable Methods, Open Physical Questions

The Quantized Dimensional Ledger (QDL) research program develops exact dimensional-closure mathematics, reproducible computational constructions, and structural-admissibility methods for physical and measurement models.

A separate foundational investigation asks whether hypothetical Quantized Dimensional Cells (QDCs) could underlie particle phenomena, collective dynamics, and spacetime. That physical hypothesis has not been independently confirmed.

The program therefore separates what has been mathematically established, implemented in software, conditionally modeled, and measured in nature.

The following statements are exact within explicitly defined QDL mathematical structures. They are not claims that nature must obey every declared closure restriction.

Five-Axis Primitive and Rank-Four Closure

Represent the declared ledger as an integer vector:

w = (L₁,L₂,L₃; F₁,F₂) ∈ ℤ⁵

Define strict closure:

C(w) = 2(L₁+L₂+L₃) − 3(F₁+F₂) = 0

Every solution has a unique integer parametrization:

w = (k+p, k+q, k−p−q; k+t, k−t)
k,p,q,t ∈ ℤ

Consequently the projected sums are (3k,2k), corresponding to the dimensional monomial L³F² raised to an integer power.

The five entries are typed ledger coordinates; they are not a claim of five measured spacetime dimensions. The full rank-four closure is distinct from the calculator's stricter default subgroup w=k(1,1,1,1,1).

Native Roots and Reversible Contacts

For relative variables (p,q,t), the selected positive quadratic form is:

Q(p,q,t) = p² + pq + q² + t²

The unit shell Q=1 contains exactly eight integer roots: six in the A₂-type relative L sector and two in the A₁-type relative F sector.

For a permitted relative transfer δ, CONTACT-37 defines:

Tδ² = I

The pair sum x+y is conserved. Within this algebra, an explicit three-contact word performs complete exchange of the two ledger states.

Reversibility and conservation are mathematical properties of this rule, not independent evidence that physical QDC contacts occur.

Phase IV — Emergent Universality

Phase IV investigates whether distinct microscopic contact choices can yield a common nontrivial physical long-wavelength theory. This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated QDC universality class.

The tested Gaussian/quadratic contact-family models exhibited a conditionally shared bulk wave equation while retaining a nontrivial relative L/F junction parameter. Observable junction scattering was therefore not uniquely selected.

Conditional bulk equation: ẅ + c² ℒGw = 0

A specified junction family yields a parameter-dependent response, for example:

P(r) = 2r / (3+r)²

In the tested model class, nontrivial junction responses did not become common in the long-wavelength limit; positive junction stiffness produced only trivial total reflection in the relevant limit.

Research disposition: MPD, CONTACT-37, CHC, QDL-EU-01 and EU-01F are retained as mathematical checkpoints. Further speculative Gaussian contact variants are on HOLD without an independently justified carrier or interaction.

This conclusion applies to the explicitly tested model family. It does not rule out all conceivable microscopic theories or coarse-graining mechanisms.

Evidence Definitions and Claim Boundaries

Exact: provable within explicitly declared axioms or definitions. Conditional: depends on an additional operator, metric, action, state or regulator choice. Restricted obstruction: a limitation proved only within a specified model class. External benchmark: relevant established non-QDC physics. Physical open: required carrier, interaction or measurement connection has not been established. Independent empirical: externally measured, discriminating, appropriately controlled physical evidence.

The QDL Institute does not currently claim that QDCs have been observed, that a common microscopic law uniquely produces the Standard Model and general relativity, or that two independent QDL-specific no-fit physical predictions have been confirmed.
