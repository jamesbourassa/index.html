QDL Physics Institute

An independent research organization advancing dimensional mathematics, measurement integrity, and evidence-controlled foundations of physics.

Founded and directed by James D. Bourassa, the QDL Physics Institute develops the Quantized Dimensional Ledger (QDL), an integer-ledger mathematical framework for studying dimensional closure and structural admissibility. Its work includes a peer-reviewed journal publication, mathematical and computational research, public software, and DOI-backed open archives.

A separate long-term program investigates Quantized Dimensional Cells (QDCs) as possible constituents of a physical substrate. The proposal remains unverified and is not presented as an experimentally completed Unified Field Theory.

Mission

Make physical and measurement-model assumptions explicit, determine what follows from them, and subject the results to independent testing.

Scientific mission

Structural Admissibility and Mathematical Physics

Develop precise dimensional ledgers, closure rules, finite mathematical constructions and validation methods. Distinguish a proven property of a declared model from a physically necessary law of nature.

Preserve successful mathematical results while clearly recording unsolved physical-selection problems and tests that do not pass.

Applied mission

Metrology, Measurement Integrity and Scientific Software

Investigate whether typed quantity ledgers and declared structural rules can strengthen model auditing, measurement-chain checking, operator audits and scientific-software validation.

Require appropriate conventional comparators, reproducible test cases, uncertainty treatment and independently assessed advantages before asserting practical superiority.

Independent value: mathematical correctness and software usefulness can be investigated without accepting the QDC substrate hypothesis.

Three Separately Evaluable Research Programs

Each research track has its own evidence standard and can be reviewed by specialists in the relevant discipline.

Track A · Mathematics

Closure, Algebra and Finite Geometry

Five-axis integer closure, parametrizations, CONTACT-37 reversible contacts, finite network identities, and representation-theoretic correspondences.

Evaluation: validity, assumptions, novelty and reproducibility.

Track B · Metrology

Structural and Measurement-Model Verification

Published metrology methods, the public QDL Calculator and internally tested Measurement Integrity Engine prototypes.

Evaluation: independent benchmarks, established alternatives, false positives, missed errors and incremental value.

Track C · Foundational Hypothesis

QDC Physical Origin and Emergence

Conditional studies of particles, gauge representations, collective dynamics, gravity and a hypothetical QDC substrate.

Evaluation: independent physical carrier, justified interactions and a distinguishing experimentally testable observable.

Research Principles

The Institute's open research record is guided by evidence boundaries, traceable calculations and the willingness to preserve negative results.

Claim Separation

Exact results, model-dependent deductions, physical interpretations, software behavior and empirical confirmations are different claims.

Predeclared Evaluation

Major tests specify assumptions, controls, pass criteria and potential failure conditions before results are interpreted.

Fail-Closed Research

A failed physical gate remains failed even if useful mathematics or a stronger future test emerges from it.

Reproducibility

Where feasible, calculations are accompanied by frozen inputs, code, machine-readable outputs, verifier scripts and checksums.

Independent Comparison

Practical utility and new physics require strong conventional comparators, uncertainty controls and outside assessment.

Open Research History

DOI-backed snapshots preserve the historical progression of conclusions rather than silently rewriting unsuccessful branches.
