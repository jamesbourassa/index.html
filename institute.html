QDL Physics Institute

The QDL Physics Institute is an independent research institute based in Huntley, Illinois, USA. It develops the Quantized Dimensional Ledger (QDL) and Quantized Dimensional Cell (QDC) research program in primitive closure, structural admissibility, physical completion, metrology, reproducible computational physics, falsification, and scientific validation.

The Institute's central research question is whether a closure-first structural architecture can do more than organize dimensional relations: whether it can progressively constrain physical dynamics, particle capacity, quantum consistency, gravity, and predictive observables.

The present QDL/QDC physical program is described as a conditional unified-field candidate undergoing a formal Completion-Gate Series . It is not presented as a completed or experimentally confirmed unified field theory.

From Primitive Closure to Physical Completion

QDL began as a framework for treating dimensional structure as an admissibility problem rather than merely as bookkeeping. The present research program asks whether that structural architecture can be extended into an explicit physical theory through a selected QDC master core, constitutive completion, quantum consistency, prediction, and independent testing.

Primitive Closure → Structural Admissibility → QDL Architecture → QDC Master Core → Completion Physics → Prediction & Replication

The Institute's present objective is not to declare that this chain is complete. It is to determine, gate by gate, exactly which links can be proved, computed, uniquely selected, reproduced, falsified, or rejected.

Mission

Closure-first research with explicit proof, computation, falsification, and claim boundaries.

Scientific Mission

The Institute's mission is to:

  • Develop QDL as a mathematically explicit theory of dimensional closure and structural admissibility.
  • Determine which physical structures follow from closure and which require additional completion assumptions.
  • Develop explicit QDC recurrence, field, localized-state, and composite-state models.
  • Investigate whether one coherent completion architecture can connect matter, interactions, quantum consistency, and gravity.
  • Distinguish structural completion from unresolved amplitude and normalization selection.
  • Produce predictions that are not separately inserted into the construction.
  • Design blind, reproducible, independently testable calculations and experiments.
  • Preserve negative results and failed selectors as part of the scientific record.
  • Publish open, DOI-backed research and reproducibility archives.
Applied Mission

The Institute also investigates whether the structural admissibility methodology can provide useful validation tools independently of the strongest QDL/QDC fundamental-physics claims.

  • Metrology and physical-constant interpretation.
  • Measurement-chain integrity.
  • Model pre-verification.
  • Scientific software validation.
  • EFT / SMEFT operator auditing.
  • AI-generated scientific-output checking.
  • Sensor-fusion and digital-twin validation.
  • Reproducible benchmark architecture.
The practical validation program can be tested on its own merits. Its usefulness does not logically require the entire QDL/QDC unified-field program to succeed.

Research Principles

The procedural commitments guiding the current program.

1. Claim separation Exact theorem, conditional theorem, finite-model result, constitutive admission, interpretation, prediction, and empirical claim are not treated as interchangeable.
2. Preregistered gates Major completion tests specify assumptions, controls, success criteria, and failure conditions before execution whenever practicable.
3. Fail-closed evaluation A failed strong gate remains failed even when it reveals useful mathematics or suggests a better next method.
4. Reproducibility Computational work is increasingly preserved through frozen source bundles, manifests, checksums, verifier scripts, and machine-readable outputs.
5. Falsifiability The goal is not merely to accommodate known physics, but to expose places where the QDL/QDC construction can fail.
6. Open research record DOI-backed public archives preserve both positive results and nonselection boundaries so later readers can reconstruct the evolution of the program.

Program Orientation

QDL is not framed as a replacement for established quantum field theory, general relativity, metrology, statistics, or experiment.

The research asks whether closure and admissibility provide an additional structural layer capable of selecting or excluding physical constructions before downstream fitting.

Where the QDL/QDC program makes stronger claims about fundamental physics, those claims are separated into explicit theorem, completion, quantum, predictive, and replication gates.

The Institute currently presents QDL/QDC as a conditional unified-field candidate. A uniquely normalized final theory and distinctive experimental confirmation have not yet been obtained.

Collaboration & Scientific Scrutiny

Independent checking is a central next step for the program.

The QDL Physics Institute welcomes collaboration and critical engagement from researchers, mathematicians, physicists, computational scientists, experimental groups, metrology laboratories, editors, reviewers, scientific-software developers, and validation organizations.

Particularly valuable forms of engagement include:

  • Independent checking of mathematical proofs and derivations.
  • Independent execution of frozen computational gates.
  • Replication of published QDL/QDC model fingerprints.
  • Review of chiral-measure and lattice-gauge constructions.
  • Alternative derivations of unresolved completion selectors.
  • Precision experiments capable of testing distinctive predictions.
  • Metrology and measurement-chain pilot applications.
  • Scientific-software validation studies.
  • Operator-audit and effective-theory comparisons.
  • External evaluation of practical structural-admissibility tools.
Critical results are welcome. An independent failure that identifies a genuine mathematical, computational, or empirical obstruction is scientifically useful and should be incorporated into the public research boundary.

For scientific correspondence, collaboration, replication, or institutional discussion: james.bourassa@qdlphysics.org .

Institutional statement
The Institute's objective is not simply to construct a unified picture, but to determine whether that picture survives disciplined scientific closure.

QDL/QDC has developed a substantial architecture, an expanding physical-completion program, reproducible computational gates, explicit particle and gravity capacity, and a growing public evidence record.

The remaining task is the decisive one: determine which parts are mathematically necessary, which require additional physical input, which generate genuinely distinctive predictions, and which survive independent scrutiny.

Until those requirements are met, the Institute describes QDL/QDC as a conditional unified-field candidate , not as completed fundamental physics.
