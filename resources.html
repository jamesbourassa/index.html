How to Interpret the V2.2 Research

V2.2 is a comprehensive theoretical research checkpoint, not an experimentally proven Unified Field Theory.

Its mathematical identities, finite constructions, and computational results can be examined under their stated assumptions.

The following physical questions remain open:

  • Independent identification of a physical QDC-like constituent or equivalent carrier.
  • A justified microscopic interaction law with physical amplitudes and phases.
  • A physically determined state, clock, and normalization.
  • Complete recovery of observed particle and gravitational dynamics.
  • Independent confirmation of a distinctive QDL-specific prediction.

The proposed cross-scale program is scientifically separate from proving the existence of microscopic QDCs.

Zenodo publication does not itself imply journal peer review or independent experimental validation.

Independent Technical Review: Four Evaluation Areas

The QDL Physics Institute welcomes rigorous written assessment. Each research area has distinct evidence requirements; no reviewer needs to accept the QDC physical hypothesis to evaluate the mathematics, software, or cross-scale methods.

A. Formal Mathematical Audit

Verify integer closure, lattice parametrization, native roots, reversible contact identities, finite-network certificates, and representation correspondences.

Compare the constructions with existing mathematical literature to assess novelty.

B. Measurement Integrity and Software

Compare a frozen QDL implementation with established dimensional-analysis, quantity-kind, semantic-type, and scientific-workflow validators.

Use independently adjudicated examples, false-positive measures, missed-error rates, and reproducible audit traces.

C. Cross-Scale Independent Testing

Evaluate whether a fixed QDL mapping provides additional invariants, error detection, or predictions across different physical systems.

Require meaningful conventional baselines, invariance under valid representation changes, and independently collected data.

D. Foundational Physics Evidence

Examine whether any physical carrier, interaction law, state, clock, or observable follows independently from the mathematical construction.

Distinguish conditional models from empirically validated physical predictions.

Review objective: a documented technical result that identifies successful reproductions, remaining assumptions, negative findings, and necessary next tests.

Exact Mathematics: Compact Reviewer Reference

The following identities are mathematically exact within the defined QDL framework. They do not establish the existence of physical Quantized Dimensional Cells.

Integer Closure and Projection

For the declared five-coordinate ledger, the closure condition is:

C(w) = 2(L₁+L₂+L₃) − 3(F₁+F₂) = 0

Every integer vector satisfying this condition is uniquely parametrized as:

w = (k+p,k+q,k−p−q;k+t,k−t)
k,p,q,t ∈ ℤ

The complete integer kernel has rank four. Its projected coordinate sums are:

(ΣL,ΣF) = (3k,2k)

The associated dimensional class is expressed through integer powers of the QDL primitive L³F².

Native Roots, Contacts and Finite Geometry

The selected relative quadratic form is:

Q(p,q,t) = p² + pq + q² + t²

The Q=1 integer shell contains exactly eight roots: six A₂-type relative L roots and two A₁-type relative F roots.

CONTACT-37 defines a reversible, sum-conserving pair transformation:

Tδ(x,y) = (y+δ,x−δ)
Tδ² = I

A specified finite network contains 16 vertices, 27 edges, and 12 independent cycles.

Its declared 30-loop frame satisfies:

ΣC C Cᵀ = 56 I₃

These properties concern specified mathematical constructions, not independently observed microscopic physical structures.

What Must Be Demonstrated?

Each proposed application must begin with a physically justified system definition.

The evaluation must specify:

  • Observable variables and physical units
  • Quantity kinds and appropriate reference frames
  • The ledger representation and mapping rule
  • The selected structural closure condition
  • Valid unit and coordinate invariance
  • Applicable conventional physical models
  • A predeclared quantitative test or defect target
  • Independent comparison data

Scientific objective: demonstrate an additional prediction, constraint, or error-detection capability not already supplied by established methods.

The cross-scale program is a research proposal. No independently confirmed universal cross-scale QDL law is claimed.

Recommended Cross-Scale Validation Sequence

Stage Required action Decision condition
1. Physical definition Specify observables, units, model boundaries, conservation laws, and measurement procedures. Reject unsupported or ambiguous physical assignments.
2. QDL mapping Declare the five-coordinate ledger, mapping procedure, and closure rule before examining target outcomes. Reject post hoc maps chosen merely to force a successful result.
3. Conventional benchmark Compare QDL with dimensional analysis, conservation laws, and relevant standard physical equations. Stop if the QDL conclusion adds no nontrivial information.
4. Prospective discriminator Freeze an independent quantitative prediction or structural error-detection test. Hold if there is no distinguishable testable consequence.
5. Independent replication Apply the frozen method to new data, and, when appropriate, another system or physical scale. Report success, failure, uncertainty, or nontransferability.
Early-rejection principle: QDL's cross-scale applications must be evaluated against conventional mathematical and physical methods. A formal closed loop, dimensional resemblance, or successful fit to known data is not sufficient evidence of new physics. The program may have value as a structural validation method even if a distinctive microscopic QDC law is never established.
